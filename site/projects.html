Projects & Research Areas

Two funded projects are the lab's current focus: evaluating whether conversational AI can give trustworthy guidance on depression treatment, and studying how patients describe chronic pain in their own words. Funded Projects

Conversational AI for Depression Management

Funded by the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute (PCORI), $1,049,998, 3 years

With Dr. Farrokh Alemi, we are evaluating conversational AI for depression management: exploring how large language models can improve antidepressant recommendations and patient outcomes. The project develops patient simulations and risk-assessment methods to test whether conversational agents give trustworthy guidance on antidepressant selection. The same causal-AI methodology has also been applied beyond depression, to COVID-19 clinical decision support. Details on the PCORI website and in the George Mason announcement.

  • Alemi et al., Causal AI Reasoning: Direct Effects Multiplicative Inference Algorithm (2026, under review)
  • Shawon et al., Advancing AI Trustworthiness Through Patient Simulation (JMIR AI, under review)
  • Lin et al., Causal Networks Guiding LLMs: Application to COVID-19 (2025)

Understanding Chronic Pain through Language

NIH-funded collaboration, $4.65 million (multi-team)

The lab is part of a George Mason research team studying chronic knee pain, using motivational interviewing and information extraction to capture patients' lived experiences with chronic pain in their own words. Our work develops annotation methods and extraction models for these interview transcripts, including new annotation frameworks for the ambiguous, biopsychosocial content the interviews surface. Details in the George Mason announcement.

  • Elyazori et al., Capturing Patients' Lived Experiences with Chronic Pain (CL4Health @ NAACL, 2025)
  • Abdulrazzaq et al., Capturing the Lived Experiences of Individuals with Chronic Pain (NCUR poster, 2026)
  • Abdulrazzaq et al., Extracting Biopsychosocial Insights from Motivational Interview Transcripts (NCUR poster, 2026)
