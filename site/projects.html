Projects

Our projects apply natural language processing and AI to health information: extracting it from clinical text, simplifying it for patients, and building conversational systems that use it responsibly.

Conversational AI for Depression Management

Funded by the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute (PCORI), $1,049,998, 3 years

With Dr. Farrokh Alemi, we are evaluating conversational AI for depression management: exploring how large language models can improve antidepressant recommendations and patient outcomes. The project develops patient simulations and risk-assessment methods to test whether conversational agents give trustworthy guidance on antidepressant selection. Details on the PCORI website and in the George Mason announcement.

AI Chatbot for Depression Care in African American Communities

Funded by an NIH AIM-AHEAD grant, $70,906

George Mason's College of Public Health received NIH AIM-AHEAD funding to develop and pilot an AI chatbot that recommends antidepressants for African Americans with depression, addressing gaps in personalized treatment. The lab contributes the NLP and conversational modeling. Details in the George Mason announcement.

Understanding Chronic Pain through Language

NIH-funded collaboration, $4.65 million (multi-team)

The lab is part of a George Mason research team studying chronic knee pain, using motivational interviewing and information extraction to capture patients' lived experiences with chronic pain in their own words. Our work develops annotation methods and extraction models for these interview transcripts. Details in the George Mason announcement.

Extracting Social Determinants of Health

Ongoing research line

We develop corpora and models for extracting social determinants of health (SDOH) from clinical notes, including the Social History Annotated Corpus and the 2022 n2c2/UW shared task on SDOH extraction. This work spans annotation methodology, event extraction, and the integration of extracted SDOH into electronic health records.

Health Text Simplification

Ongoing research line

We build annotated corpora and models that simplify complex health information for patients, including work on digestive cancer education materials and evaluation of health question answering under readability constraints.

Clinical Information Extraction from Imaging Reports

Ongoing research line

With collaborators at the University of Washington, we develop annotated corpora and extraction models for radiology and other imaging reports, including fine-grained event-based annotations, follow-up identification, and incidental finding detection.
